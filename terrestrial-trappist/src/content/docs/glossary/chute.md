---
title: Chute / Wave / Leftover Chute
description: BeeSort terms for the physical bins and the batches of work fed through them.
---

Three [BeeSort](/glossary/beesorter/) terms that describe where a package physically lands and how the work is batched:

- A **chute** is a physical bin the machine sorts a package into. Each chute is assigned one [route code](/glossary/route-code/) by the operator, so everything landing in it belongs to the same zone, service area, or route.
- A **wave** — a sortation session — is one batch of zones processed together. A single BeeSort device has more routes to serve than it has chutes, so ops work through the zones in successive waves rather than all at once.
- A **leftover chute** catches packages whose route is not part of the current wave. They are not errors; they are simply early. The operator re-feeds them in a later wave once their route has a chute.

A typical device is configured with 33 usable chutes for route assignment plus two reserved leftover chutes (34 and 35), though both counts are per-device settings rather than fixed constants.
